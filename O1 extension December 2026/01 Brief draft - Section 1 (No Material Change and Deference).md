## 1. Request; No Material Change Since the October 13, 2026 Approval

**The request.** Octet asks USCIS to extend Mr. Devani's stay from December 14, 2026 through December 13, 2029. The Policy Manual provides for an extension of up to three years where the beneficiary will pursue a new event or activity (8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(12)(ii); USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 2, Part M, Ch. 9). Section 2 sets out that new event, the commercial launch and scale-up of Octet's rebuilt Grapevine platform, together with its itinerary. If USCIS finds that any part of the requested period does not qualify, Petitioner asks in the alternative that USCIS approve the extension for the longest period it finds supported, and in any event for not less than one year.

**USCIS reviewed this employment on the full record weeks ago.** On August 28, 2026, USCIS issued a Request for Evidence on Petition IOE8795688197. In it, USCIS declined to defer to its 2023 approval. It asked for evidence of Mr. Devani's maintenance of status, including the deferral of his cash salary. It also asked for evidence of his eligibility under each criterion Petitioner relied on. Petitioner answered each point. On October 13, 2026, USCIS approved the petition (Exhibit 1.1). That approval followed a review of the merits, not reliance on an earlier decision.

**Nothing material has changed since then.** This petition continues the employment USCIS approved on October 13, 2026, with the same petitioner, in the same position, on the same terms:

The terms and conditions of Mr. Devani's employment are therefore unchanged within the meaning of 8 C.F.R. § 214.2(o)(2)(iv)(D). USCIS considered the deferral specifically in its Request for Evidence and approved the petition with it in place. Since that approval Mr. Devani has remained in O-1A status, working full time for Octet in the approved position and for no one else (Exhibits [I-797A, I-94, declaration]).

The new business project described at Section 2 changes none of these terms. Mr. Devani will hold the same office, perform the same duties and draw the same salary. What is new is the phase of Octet's business that his work will serve. The Policy Manual recognises exactly this distinction: it treats continuing "in a different phase or trial for the same research" as a new event. The same work in a new phase is a new event or activity for the purposes of the validity period. It is not a change in the terms of employment.

**Deference.** Under 8 C.F.R. § 214.1(c)(5), when USCIS adjudicates a Form I-129 request involving the same parties and the same underlying facts, it gives deference to its prior determination of eligibility. It need not do so only where the prior approval involved a material error, there has been a material change in circumstances or eligibility requirements, or there is new material information that adversely affects eligibility. [Verify against the current CFR text before filing.] None applies:
- **No material error.** The October 13, 2026 approval rests on a record that USCIS itself requested and reviewed.
- **No material change.** The table above shows the terms are unchanged.
- **No new adverse information.** Petitioner knows of none.

Petitioner respectfully asks USCIS to defer to its determination of October 13, 2026 that Mr. Devani qualifies for O-1A classification. If USCIS relies on a specific change or new information, Petitioner respectfully asks USCIS to identify it so that Petitioner may respond.

Petitioner understands that DHS has proposed to remove 8 C.F.R. § 214.1(c)(5) (Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, 91 Fed. Reg. [page], October 14, 2026). As of filing that proposal is not final, and the regulation remains in effect. So that USCIS may decide this petition on any footing without delay, and without waiving the request for deference, Petitioner resubmits at Section 3 the evidence of eligibility on which USCIS approved the petition in October 2026.
